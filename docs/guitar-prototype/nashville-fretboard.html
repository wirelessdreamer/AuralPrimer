Nashville numbers on the fretboard

Positions are geometry; numbers come from the key. The five pentatonic boxes are places on the neck. Inside a box every dot gets its number in the song's key, in three tiers: the box's five notes, the two that complete the scale, and notes outside the key, drawn only when the song plays them. Switch Count from to see the same box read from the major tonic (the box root is 6) or from its relative minor (the box root is 1).

All five boxes across the neck, numbered with the blues/rock convention: position 1 is the minor box with its root on the low E string. The selected one shows every tier. Click a box to select it.

Melody inside a position

A four-bar phrase in position 1 that moves to position 2 in bar 3 and bends into a blue note. Three ways to read it.

Box with lit numbers

The box stays drawn; each note lights up on its dot with its number, and the next one counts down in place. The view slides when the phrase changes position.

Best for learning where a number lives. Fast runs blur together.

Number tab

Tab lines, but each note carries its number instead of a fret, with the position marked above. Inside a known box, a string and a number name exactly one dot.

Reads like a chart. Needs the box in your head, so it suits players who already know it.

Lanes with numbers

The string-lanes highway with numbers on the gems and the box on the strip below, so the hand position stays visible.

Fits band mode. The number and the place are in two spots.

Triads inside the position

A 1 5 6m 4 progression as three-string triads, voiced inside whichever position is selected. Pick another position and they re-voice. The box stays faint behind.

Scale numbers

Each triad note shows its number in the key. The 4 chord reads 4 6 1, so the chord is visibly built from the box.

Keeps one language with the melody. Hides which note is the chord's root.

Chord tones

Each note shows its job in the chord: R, 3 (♭3 for minor) and 5. The chord's number sits beside the shape.

Teaches inversions. Loses the link back to the melody's numbers.

Both at once

The dot carries the scale number; its ring colour carries the chord tone: cyan root, rose third, violet fifth.

The most information. Needs the colour key learned once.

Barre chords as they are

The same progression as full barre chords, each taking the E or A shape nearest the selected position. Each card says which box the barre overlaps most. The 6m barre is position 1's own root shape.

Shape and number

The barre as a plain shape, with the chord's Nashville number large beside it. No numbers on the dots.

Closest to how barres are played: one grip, one number.

Numbers on the shape

Every dot in the barre shows its number in the key, so the shape and the box share one language.

Busy: six numbers per chord, most of them repeats.

Chord tones on the shape

R, 3 and 5 on the barre, roots highlighted, plus the chord's number. Shows where the roots are for moving the grip.

Useful for switching between E and A shapes; the key is only in the big number.

Questions these raise

  1. Default count in a major-key song. Count from the major 1 (position 1's root reads 6) or from the relative minor? This page defaults to the major 1, since charts are written that way.
  2. Spelling notes outside a major key. This page writes a blue note as ♭3 and ♭7. The app's current table writes the same notes as #2 and #6 in sharp keys, which reads wrong to most players.
  3. What moves the box. The chart's hand positions, the player's live position from the Jamstik, or the chord? Here the melody carries its own position, and triads and barres follow the selected position.
  4. Barre labels. Shape and number for rhythm parts, with numbers on the dots only in a practice view?
